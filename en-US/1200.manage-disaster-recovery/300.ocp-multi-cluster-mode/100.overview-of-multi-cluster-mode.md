# Overview of multi-cluster mode

OCP supports multi-cluster mode, enabling a high-availability disaster recovery architecture across two IDCs. This provides you with more reliable cluster management and monitoring services.

<main id="notice" type='notice'>
<h4>Applicability</h4>
<p>This topic applies only to OCP Enterprise Edition. OCP Community Edition does not support this feature. </p>
</main>

## Overview

The multi-cluster mode of OCP is designed for cross-region disaster recovery scenarios, providing centralized management capabilities for primary and standby databases across different regions. This mode supports the complete lifecycle management of operations and maintenance, including the creation, decoupling, upgrade, routine switching, and disaster recovery drills of primary and standby databases, ensuring business continuity and data security.

The multi-cluster management feature of OCP is primarily aimed at unified disaster recovery management of primary and standby databases across different OCP clusters. Its core function is to synchronize topology information between primary and standby databases, ensuring that disaster recovery switching and business recovery can be performed quickly in the event of a data center-level failure. It is important to note that this feature does not include automatic failover. When the primary OCP cluster fails, the standby OCP cluster does not automatically take over the resources managed by the primary OCP cluster. In the primary-standby OCP architecture, there is no data synchronization link between MetaDB (metadata database) instances, and they remain independent in terms of data storage. Their coordination is mainly achieved through data transmission and business logic at the application layer.

<main id="notice" type='explain'>
<h4>Note</h4>
<p>Before OceanBase Database V4.1.0, the primary and standby databases were at the cluster level. Starting from OceanBase Database V4.1.0, the primary and standby databases are at the tenant level. That is, the primary or standby role information belongs to the tenant, which is divided into primary and standby tenants. The cluster no longer has the concept of primary and standby roles but serves as a container for tenants.</p>
</main>

## Architecture of the multi-cluster mode

The OCP multi-cluster disaster recovery switching feature is primarily used in scenarios such as cross-city dual data centers and cross-city triple data centers. The principle is as follows:

### Cross-city dual data centers

In a cross-city primary-standby database deployment scenario, you can use the cross-city dual data center deployment solution of the OCP cluster. When the OCP cluster and the OceanBase cluster it manages in one city fail, the OCP cluster in the standby city needs to be switched to the primary cluster. The primary-standby disaster recovery switching mechanism is then used to take over the operations, monitoring, and alerting functions of the OceanBase database, ensuring that the database service is restored to a usable state as quickly as possible.

The primary and standby OCP clusters are deployed in City 1 and City 2, respectively. The OCP cluster in City 1 is the primary cluster, and the OCP cluster in City 2 is the standby cluster. Each OCP cluster manages multiple OceanBase clusters.

When a network failure causes the connection between the two cities to be interrupted, you can perform a disaster recovery switch to switch the OCP cluster in City 2 to the primary cluster. At this point, the original primary OCP cluster in City 1 becomes an independent and obsolete cluster. It is recommended to delete this obsolete cluster and set up a new primary cluster in City 1. The primary OCP cluster in City 2 will continue to perform operations and maintenance and monitoring on the OceanBase database. For standby databases whose primary database is not managed by this cluster, it will perform disaster recovery switching or migration, as described in [Switch an OCP cluster in a failover](600.switch-an-ocp-cluster-in-a-failover.md).

For OceanBase clusters of versions earlier than V4.1.0, businesses access the OceanBase cluster by associating it with an OBProxy. For OceanBase primary-standby clusters across OCP clusters, each OceanBase cluster is associated with an OBProxy. If the OBProxy is associated with the standby database of the business cluster, the business can access the standby database of the business cluster. If a network failure occurs between the primary and standby OCP clusters, the business can continue to access the OceanBase cluster through the OBProxy associated with the standby cluster.

<main id="notice" type='explain'>
<h4>Note</h4>
<p>If the OBProxy is associated only with the standby cluster, the username must include the standby cluster ID when accessing through the proxy, for example, <code>mysql -h xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx -P 2881 -u&lt;username&gt;@&lt;tenant name#cluster name&gt;:1000 -p ****</code>. The cluster ID is also displayed in the connection string of the OCP cluster. If the ID is removed, the business cannot access the standby database of the business cluster.</p>
</main>

After the network is restored, the two primary clusters continue to operate independently. You can set one of the primary clusters as the standby cluster and bind the two clusters by registering the OCP cluster, as described in [Register an OCP cluster](200.register-an-ocp-cluster.md).

### Cross-city triple data centers

In a cross-city triple data center scenario, OCP clusters are deployed in City 1 and City 2, respectively. The OCP cluster in City 1 is the primary cluster, and the two OCP clusters in City 2 are the standby clusters. Each OCP cluster manages multiple OceanBase clusters.

When a network failure causes the connection between the two cities to be interrupted, you can perform a disaster recovery switch to switch one of the standby OCP clusters to the primary cluster. At this point, there is one primary OCP cluster in City 1 and one primary OCP cluster and one standby OCP cluster in City 2.

Each primary OCP cluster continues to perform operations and maintenance and monitoring on the OceanBase databases it manages. If the primary database of a standby database is not managed by the current primary OCP cluster, you need to clear the data of the standby database, as described in [Switch an OCP cluster in a failover](600.switch-an-ocp-cluster-in-a-failover.md).

For OceanBase clusters of versions earlier than V4.1.0, businesses access the OceanBase cluster by associating it with an OBProxy. For OceanBase primary-standby clusters across OCP clusters, each OceanBase cluster is associated with an OBProxy. If the OBProxy is associated with the standby database of the business cluster, the business can access the standby database of the business cluster. If a network failure occurs between the primary and standby OCP clusters, the business can continue to access the OceanBase cluster through the OBProxy associated with the standby cluster.

<main id="notice" type='explain'>
<h4>Note</h4>
<p>If the OBProxy is associated only with the standby cluster, the username must include the standby cluster ID when accessing through the proxy, for example, <code>mysql -h xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx -P 2881 -u&lt;username&gt;@&lt;tenant name#cluster name&gt;:1000 -p ****</code>. The cluster ID is also displayed in the connection string of the OCP cluster. If the ID is removed, the business cannot access the standby database of the business cluster.</p>
</main>

After the network is restored, the standby database of City 2 will appear in the list of OCP clusters in City 1. However, the primary database of this standby database is the primary cluster in City 2. You can unbind the standby database and remove it, as described in [Delete an OCP cluster](700.delete-an-ocp-cluster.md).

If you want to rejoin the original primary cluster to the OCP cluster, you need to clear the data in the original primary cluster.
